# Nepal Landslide Disaster Relief Donation Campaign

## Disaster Overview

On August 26, 2026, a severe natural disaster struck the Nepal-Tibet border region. A massive landslide and flood triggered by glacier collapse swept through dozens of villages and settlements along approximately 72 kilometers of the Trishuli River (also known as the Bhotekoshi River).

### Disaster Statistics (Continuously Updated)

- **Death Toll**: At least 584 people (579 in Nepal, 5 in Tibet)
- **Missing**: 1,942 people (including hundreds of foreign tourists, 33 Americans among them)
- **Affected Population**: Over 93,000 people in urgent need of humanitarian assistance
- **Affected Areas**: Rasuwa and Nuwakot districts of Nepal (Timure and Syapru Besi worst hit)
- **Infrastructure Damage**: At least 19 bridges destroyed, approximately 40-45 km of roads washed away, multiple hydropower plants damaged
- **Disaster Magnitude**: Tremor from glacier collapse equivalent to a 5.2 magnitude earthquake

### Cause of Disaster

According to scientific analysis, this disaster was caused by glacier collapse triggered by climate change. Massive amounts of ice and rock slid down from the Himalayas, forming powerful mudslides and floods that destroyed villages, homes, bridges, and power stations along the river.
